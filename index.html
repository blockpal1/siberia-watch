Diagnosis conflict
No new official diagnosis. Governor Kobzev (Telegram, Mon Oct 5) now repeats the federal line: the lab worker’s cause of death was “pneumonia of unspecified etiology,” with no microorganisms linked to her professional work, no new institute staff seeking care in recent days, and the situation “stable” and “under control.” Kremlin spokesman Peskov (Oct 5) told reporters to rely on Rospotrebnadzor, not “rumors.” That official pneumonia line is not a clearance. Plague reports remain in conflict with it: Buryatia head Tsydenov first said plague, then edited his post to “maybe” plague; Lyudi Baikala reported a broken tube with plague bacteria on Sep 25. New: the Baikalsk town administration asked people on Oct 3 not to travel to Shelekhov “because of plague,” then deleted the post (Euronews RU). Time notes some Russian media reports are no longer online. This page still counts 1 index death under contested etiology. It does not treat the death as a cleared case or as a confirmed plague case count.
Exposure
Earlier reporting described a lab-linked exposure around Sep 25, 2026, at the Irkutsk Anti-Plague Institute. The index patient is the lab worker.
Contacts
Still about 197 / nearly 200 under observation (no new headcount). Daily lab testing continues (Shelekhov district mayor Modin). Contact screen unchanged: 2 COVID-19 and 2 rhinovirus cases, no other pathogens. WHO says, per reports, no contact has shown symptoms to date. RFE/RL Siberia.Realities (Oct 5, anonymous sources) says more than 60 anti-plague institute staff have been confined inside the institute since Oct 2, with no positive test so far. That is a sourced, unofficial claim, not an official figure. No secondary case.
Geography
Unchanged ring: Shelekhov / Irkutsk. Shelekhov district hospital inpatient quarantine continues; the Shelekhov polyclinic is working normally. Moscow Times (Oct 5) says local media report area hospitals running on standard schedules apart from localized precautions, and IrCity’s Oct 5 visits to Irkutsk City Hospitals No. 1, 3, 10, and 9 found normal operations. Svoboda / Lyudi Baikala patient accounts of blocked Irkutsk hospitals remain in conflict. That conflict stays visible. No Buryatia cases. No export or second-country case.
Official bulletins
New: first WHO statement on this event (to Newsweek and CNBC, Oct 5). WHO says it is aware of reports that a lab worker in Irkutsk oblast died of severe pneumonia on Oct 2; no cause of death has been officially confirmed, and laboratory testing is reportedly underway. Based on unofficial information, the risk to the general population “appears to be low,” to be updated. WHO is in contact with Russian authorities under IHR Articles 9 and 10 to verify the event, the lab tests, the contact-tracing rationale, and any international implications, and has offered support. This is a request to verify, not a WHO case confirmation, outbreak notice, or emergency declaration. Also new: Secretary of State Rubio (Oct 5) said the US is “watching and monitoring it closely”; the Washington Post (four anonymous officials) reports CDC, State, ASPR, and other agencies are working to learn more; a European Commission spokesperson says the EU is monitoring “very closely.” Earlier State Department and CDC monitoring statements stand. Still no CDC case bulletin. The Russian federal pneumonia line is unchanged.
